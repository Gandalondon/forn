## Batch 1 review (21 Nov, Tony)
- 12 kept, 8 rejected: 40%, above the 1 in 5 bar. Answers also saved in the review page's store (collection review-batch-1).
- Rejected: black-buddha, henman-hill, caesars-camp, john-innes-compost, mosque-chimney, bazalgette-tomb, ophelia-hogsmill, wimbledon-pro-colour.
- The pattern: every note says the notification line works but the card doesn't. 7 of 8 rejects were Confusing and 7 were Too long. Only black-buddha was Dull. The weak spot is the writing, not the choice of facts.
- Writing rules from batch 1 (to add to RULES.md at the next push):
  - The card follows on from the notification. Use the notification's key word (mosque, sewers, painting) and answer it in the first sentence.
  - Aim for under 30 words. Two short sentences, never three ideas.
  - Say what a thing is: "the painting Ophelia", "the Pope's ambassador". No terms he'd have to look up (Great Stink, ordination hall, barrows, male professionals).
  - Don't use precise ranges or hedges when a rounder fact is true (650 to 700 years becomes "nearly 700").
  - If the card says something happened, give the cause when the source has it. If the source doesn't, don't raise the question.
  - Added 21 Nov: the notification line gets the same claim check as the card (check.py only checks the card).
  - Added 21 Nov (batch 3): no tacked-on second fact. A card's second sentence explains or extends the first; it never starts a new topic (Clattern Bridge: the football goal plus "scolds were ducked from it too" confused him, and "scolds" is a term he'd have to look up).
  - Added 21 Nov (batch 4): name the kind of thing in plain words in both the line and the card, even when the proper name seems to say it: "the band Madness", "the Dublin Castle, a Camden pub", "an English army", "St Stephen's Church", "an oak tree". A name alone (Edmund Ironside, the Konrads, St Stephen's) isn't enough. Cut extra names rather than explain them.
- All 40 rewritten to these rules (29 changed). The scores stay the same, except black-buddha goes from 7 to 6. Rechecked against the sources: still claim-for-claim, the longest card is 38 words and the average is about 27. content/facts/sw20-merton-draft.json is now the rewritten set.
